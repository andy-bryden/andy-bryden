# 1 — The Lion

Byzantium, 232 AD.

Sand kicked up under bronze-shod feet as Thana and Toria closed the distance at a dead sprint, blades already rising, the crowd's roar loud enough to drown whichever of them screamed first.

Then a second roar cut under it — deeper, guttural, rolling in off the far side of the arena — and both women broke stride at once, blades still up, eyes cutting toward the sound instead of each other.

A lion came through the gate, mane low, claws tearing sand, and every eye in the stands went with it.

Servius had owned and trained more than one gladiatrix in his years running the Kynegion, but the Greek woman, Thana, was the only one who had ever really riled him. He had grown tired of her arrogance. Tired of the smirk she wore into every bout, the one that said she already knew how the other woman would die before the first blow landed. Eleven fights, eleven corpses that weren't hers, and still that same insufferable calm.

This day, Servius had decided to pair her against the Hispanic firebrand, Toria.

He had never matched two of his own gladiatrices against each other before. It wasn't done, not when both women drew a crowd and both crowds paid. But the sun-bronzed Iberian was fast in a way Thana wasn't, vicious in a way the Greek's cold precision had never had to answer for, and he told himself the novelty alone would cover the loss. Two gladiatrices, one blade each, armor same as any other bout. Let the smirk answer that.

What he hadn't accounted for was the third woman.

Tacita was difficult to manage in ways that had nothing to do with defiance. She never argued, never resisted, never gave the guards a reason to raise a hand. She moved through the compound quietly, often somewhere she wasn't supposed to be. She had been there three years, and not once had Servius heard her utter a word, or even make a sound. In that time she had also learned to move silently, which shouldn't have been possible in bronze armor. There was something uncanny about her he could never put a finger on.

While Thana and Toria were led into the tunnel that opened onto the sand, Tacita was already gone, slipping into the low stone corridor where the compound kept its beasts.

She had a plan, and it was not a subtle one.

Back in the arena, the lion paused momentarily, sizing up the two women, neither of whom had been primed for fighting a beast this round. But they had both participated in paired animal kills before, and their tactics switched immediately. They needed to divide the lion's attention to gain the advantage. Without a word, and not even so much as a second glance towards each other, they peeled apart and approached from two different angles. The lion turned its focus on Thana and in a moment, Toria's falcata tore into the beast's emaciated frame. It was over in under a minute — messy, efficient. The crowd didn't see a jailbreak. They saw a miracle. They saw two gladiatrices turn a death-match into a shared triumph, and they roared for it, and Thana and Toria walked out of that arena arm in arm.

In the tunnels beneath the arena, the animal handlers were already unraveling. A lion loose mid-bout wasn't bad luck. It was a failure, and they knew exactly whose neck it would land on. Word went out fast — voices raised — calling down every man they could pull from elsewhere in the compound to help account for what had gone wrong. Guards who should have been at their posts were suddenly needed somewhere else entirely.

When Thana and Toria came back in off the sand, still catching their breath, they found Tacita waiting for them in the passage.

Tacita simply beckoned and they followed. They knew whose hand had released the lion, and they knew, too, who had cleared the passage while the crowd was busy cheering a miracle.
