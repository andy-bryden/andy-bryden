# 30 — Marcellus Is Missing

The Praefectus went down from the tower and out into the yard, where the gate stood open and the wreckage of a century was still filing through.

"Form them," he said, to the gate captain. "Get water into them. Then bring me whoever can still speak plainly."

A man near the gate, blood dried black down one side of his face, told it start to finish twice in a row, and the two tellings didn't agree with each other. Another, further in, hadn't heard any shout at all — he'd been at the horse lines the whole time, he said, and the horses had simply started dying around him, one after another. A third began an account of the centurion going down and stopped halfway through it, unable to say with any certainty whether he'd seen Marcellus fall or not.

None of it agreed. The Praefectus had heard that particular kind of disagreement for two nights running now.

He went from man to man himself rather than wait for the reports to be gathered. He wanted the accounts raw.

Slowly and unevenly, he began to piece together the story.

The recovery had been underway — four dead, his cousin's own hand on the work of bringing them in gently. Then a shout from the north, and a man down before he'd gone ten paces past the tree line, an arrow in his throat from somewhere out of sight. Then a second shout from the south and another man down — too far to be the same archer. Two separate threats, deliberately placed and deliberately timed. This had been a well planned ambush.

And then there was the horses.

It was clear from the accounts that the cavalrymen had not been the target of the attack. It was the horses that had been targeted, which was a tactic he could not fathom. Blades to the legs, a spear into a chest, one animal after another went down, its rider thrown or trapped beneath it.

In the middle of a heaving mass of screaming horseflesh and struggling cavalrymen, not one of them sparing a moment to see who was where. It was only back inside his own gate, under the light of the torches, that any of them realised that the centurion was not there.

They all now assumed that Marcellus was dead, yet not one of them would say so.

"At first light," he said. "A party goes out. Full strength, foot and horse both — what horse we have left. We bring back our dead. All of them. And we do not come back inside these walls until we know that every man is accounted for."
