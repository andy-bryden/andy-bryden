# 34 — Rome on Its Head

They had put three ridges between themselves and the fort by dusk.

"I keep thinking about the gate," Tove said, turning a piece of kindling in her hands without feeding it to the fire yet. "Not tonight's gate. Tomorrow's. The next watchman who has to stand it, knowing what stood outside it last week and walked away without anyone catching a glimpse."

"He won't sleep," Thana said. "None of them will. We didn't have to kill the whole garrison to ruin their sleep for the season — we only had to send one man back to remind the rest what waiting for us feels like." She stretched toward the fire. "I've fought plenty who feared dying. This is the first time I've built one that fears living."

Taya nodded, not looking up from the arrow she was fletching. "A man afraid of dying is done costing you anything the moment he's dead. A man afraid of living carries it into every room he sits in, for as long as he lasts. That's worth more to us than half a garrison dead."

Thana laughed. "I won't argue that."

Toria had been quiet longer than usual. "I keep thinking about tonight's report," she said. "The one the Praefectus has to write now, once his hand stops shaking enough to hold the stylus." She looked into the fire rather than at any of them. "What does a man like that even say. *I lost a patrol. I lost a woman I kept three years and never once thought could leave. I lost a century, and my own cousin's mind besides, and none of it to an enemy I can name to a superior without sounding mad.* I'd almost pity the wax."

They all laughed.

"It won't stay at Lorch," Thilda said. "Whatever he writes goes up the line to someone who's never smelled that fort's air. That man reads numbers he can't reconcile, and he has two choices. Believe it, and admit the frontier's rotting. Or doubt it, and hope the next report proves him right." She fed the fire. "Either way, he won't sleep well either. Fear moves up a chain slower than it moves down one. But it does move up."

"That's the part I like best," Tove said, leaning forward. "Not just Lorch. Whoever sits above the man who sits above the Praefectus. Some official who's never thought about a frontier fort by name, holding a dispatch that says eight women undid a century and walked a Roman officer home in a dress, and nobody can say how it was done." She shook her head. "That's not a bad week for one Praefectus anymore. That's the whole of Rome on its head."

"You're imagining a very large collapse from one very small dress," Thana said.

"Am I. Rome's whole trick is convincing everyone that who stands over whom can never change. Man over woman. Captor over captive. Citizen over everyone who isn't." Tove nodded toward Taran. "Dress one man exactly the way he'd have dressed one of us, tether him exactly the way he tethered her, and you haven't just humiliated a centurion. You've shown every man who believed in that order that it isn't fixed. It only looked fixed because nobody had yet had the nerve to turn it around on them."

Taran hadn't spoken. She sat apart from the fire's full light, the bow across her knees, thumb moving along the grain.

"I only care what it's done to a brute who used to hand me a practice blade and call it generous," she said, finally, eyes narrowing. "Three years, he stood there and let his cousin show me off like a hound that had learned to heel. Now half a garrison lies awake wondering if the thing they can't see is standing at their own door." She smiled a thin smile with no warmth in it. "Let the empire crack if it wants to. I only wanted the one house to know it's as safe as it thought."

Tove's hand found her shoulder, but no further words were exchanged.

Taya let the quiet hold a moment. "I'd be careful how satisfied we let ourselves get, all the same. A frightened system doesn't only despair. Sometimes it hunts harder, because it can't afford to be seen doubting itself. The bigger the crack we imagine we've made, the more men Rome will decide it can spend proving the crack isn't there." She set the finished arrow down. "Tonight, we've earned the fire. Tomorrow, we should assume they're already deciding how much they're willing to lose finding out we exist."

"They already know we exist," Toria said.

"They know a story exists," Taya said. "There's a difference, and it's the difference that's kept us alive this long. Let's not spend it early because it felt good to imagine the size of what we'd done."

Tiata had let the whole of it run without steering it. Only now did she weigh in.

"All of that is true, and exactly how we meant it to be," Tiata said. She let the falx settle across her knees. "But Rome doesn't come apart because of one dead century, or one broken man, or one report a Praefectus can't bring himself to write plainly. It comes apart from years of it, done over and over, by people it never thought worth watching. Tonight was one weight added to the pile. There'll be others before we're finished." She looked around the fire. "Toria said as much, the first morning I rode with any of you. Not to beat Rome in open ground. To make the men running it start doubting the ground they're already standing on."

Tacita hadn't spoken. She fed the fire, reached over once and set her hand against Taran's arm, and went back to the fire.
