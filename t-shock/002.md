# Chapter Two — Out of Byzantium

The tunnels beneath the arena were built for animals and the men who managed them, low stone and narrow turns, and in the confusion nobody had thought to watch the three women who didn't belong there.

Thana and Toria still carried what they'd fought with. No one had thought to disarm them — why would they, in the scramble to explain a loose lion, to account for a failure that would cost someone his hide — and so a falcata and whatever blade Thana had carried onto the sand came with them, unremarked, into the dark.

Tacita already had hers. Whatever she'd taken in the beast cages, on her way to the lion's gate, she'd taken quietly and without asking, the way she took everything. She didn't explain it. She never did.

She led them without hesitating, through turns that suggested she'd walked them before — maybe that same afternoon, maybe over weeks nobody had noticed. Handlers' voices carried from side passages, sharp with panic, all of it aimed at each other and none of it aimed at three gladiatrices who should have been back in a cell by now. Tacita moved through the noise like it belonged to someone else's building.

They came out through a low service door into the cool dark beyond the compound walls, the city's noise a different register out here — market close, temple bells, someone singing badly two streets over.

They had perhaps a hundred paces before the horn went up behind them.

A headcount, a door found unbarred, someone finally asking the right question at the wrong time — it didn't matter which. What mattered was the shout that followed it, and the second shout answering the first, and the sound of hobnailed sandals breaking into a run on stone.

"Move," Thana said, and they moved.

Byzantium at that hour was a maze of narrow lanes and blind corners, walls close enough on both sides to touch, and Tacita took it like she'd already memorized every wrong turn in it. She cut left where the street looked like it dead-ended, and didn't, ducked under a hanging line of drying fish, vaulted a low wall into a courtyard that spat them out three streets further than any pursuer would guess.

It wasn't enough. Byzantium's watch knew their own city too.

Two men came out of a side alley ahead of them, blades already drawn, shouting for them to halt — and Toria didn't slow down to consider it. The falcata came up and around in the same motion as her stride, catching the first man across the throat before he'd finished the word, and she was past him and into the second before he'd registered what had happened to his partner. He didn't get his blade up in time. Neither of them would be standing watch again, that night or any other.

Behind them, a third pursuer rounded the corner at a dead run and met Thana instead. She didn't have Toria's speed, but she'd never needed it — she stepped inside his reach, took the strike on her forearm instead of her chest, and put her blade in under his ribs with the same cold economy she brought to every bout she'd ever fought clean. He went down without a sound. She was already moving again before he finished falling.

More voices behind them now, closer than before, torchlight starting to bounce off the walls at the mouth of the lane. Tacita caught Toria's sleeve and hauled her sideways through a gap between two buildings barely wide enough for a shoulder, and Thana followed without asking why — there wasn't time to ask why, there was only time to run and trust that the woman who'd gotten a lion loose in a guarded arena knew how to get three women out of a guarded city.

They lost the torches in the tanners' quarter, where the smell was bad enough that even a determined watch didn't want to linger, cutting through standing water and worse until the shouting behind them thinned into confusion and then, finally, into nothing they could hear at all.

By the time Byzantium's outer wall rose up ahead of them, dark against a darker sky, the pursuit had scattered into search parties combing the wrong streets, calling to each other across distances that told all three women exactly how lost their hunters already were.

They didn't go through a gate. Tacita found a stretch of wall where the stonework had crumbled enough for a foothold, and they went over it instead, dropping into the scrub beyond breathing hard, blood on two blades that wasn't theirs.

They didn't put distance between themselves and the wall — not that first night. Distance was what a frightened person bought, and none of them mistook fear for sense. They stayed close enough to watch the gates, close enough to hear which direction the horns went out in, and used the dark to learn the ground instead of running blind across it. They slept well that night, knowing their pursuers would be expecting them to be running, not resting.

At first light, a search party came out from the city gate — four men, moving the way men move when they expect to find nothing.

Tacita spotted them before they came anywhere near spotting her. She watched them long enough to learn their spacing, then went back for the other two.

What happened after wasn't a fight so much as a correction. Toria and Thana had killed men in the dark of a city, quick and necessary; this was neither quick nor, strictly, necessary — it was a message, delivered in the same coin Byzantium had sent out to collect them. Four men went into that stretch of scrub looking for three fugitives. None of them walked back out to report what they'd found.

By the time anyone in Byzantium worked out that a search party had gone missing rather than simply failed, the three of them were already gone north, leaving no one behind who could say which way.
