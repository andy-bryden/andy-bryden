# 8 — The Cost of Silence

They made camp that night within sight of the boundary stone Tiata had thrown back into the brush, close enough that none of them had needed to discuss why. It felt, without anyone saying so, like a place worth finishing a day at rather than merely stopping in.

Tacita built the fire, as she often did — quickly, without wasted motion, the kind of competence that had never once needed a word attached to it. She had been the quietest of them since Byzantium, quieter even than the silence itself sometimes required, and by now the others had stopped noticing it as absence. It was simply how she took up space.

Tiata noticed it differently. Tiata noticed everything differently.

"You haven't spoken since I joined you," she said, once the fire had caught and the others had settled into the loose, unhurried quiet that followed a day like the one behind them. It wasn't an accusation. It landed the same way everything from Tiata landed — flat, exact, entirely without softness, and somehow more disarming for it than sympathy would have been.

Tacita didn't look up from the fire.

"Either you don't speak because you can't," Tiata went on, "or because you've decided against it. I've met both kinds. You're not the first kind."

Still nothing. But something in the set of Tacita's shoulders had changed, minutely, the way still water changes when something moves beneath it.

Thana shifted, half-ready to step in the way she sometimes did when a silence ran too long for her comfort, but Tiata lifted one hand, barely, and Thana settled again. This wasn't hers to interrupt.

"I don't ask to be cruel," Tiata said. "I ask because I've noticed you're the one among all of us who never once has to be corrected. Taya reads people. Thana reads fights. Toria acts before she thinks and is usually right anyway. But you—" she paused, choosing the words with the same care she chose everything, "—you never do anything that needs undoing. That's not nature. That's discipline. And discipline like that is built, not born, whatever quiet you started with."

The fire cracked. Nobody else breathed too loudly.

"I was already quiet," Tacita said.

The words came out low, and rough with disuse, like a door that hadn't been opened in years finally giving on its hinges. It was the first full sentence any of them had heard from her since the night they'd followed her out of Byzantium, and the shock of it moved around the fire like a current — Taya's eyes flicking up sharp, Toria going very still, Thana, for once, saying nothing at all.

Tiata simply nodded, once, as if she'd expected exactly that answer and no other. "I imagine you were. But quiet isn't silence. Not this kind." She let that sit a moment before she asked the rest of it, gently for her, which was still not gentle at all. "What made the difference?"

Tacita was quiet again for a long while. Long enough that the others thought, perhaps, that was all there would be — one sentence, hard-won, and nothing further owed to anyone. But she was still looking at the fire, not at any of them, when she finally spoke again, each word coming slower than the last, as though she were paying some private cost for every one of them.

"My father," she said. "Owed a debt he could not pay honestly." A pause, her jaw working once before the rest came. "He found a way to pay it that did not cost him anything he valued."

"Rome bought what he sold," she said, at last, flat and final, as if she were closing a door she had only opened wide enough to prove it could still move. "That is all there is."

It plainly wasn't all there was. But Tiata, who had pushed exactly as far as the question required and not one word further, simply nodded again and let it stand.

"That's enough," she said. Not dismissive. Something closer to respect.

Tacita went back to the fire, feeding it a piece of wood that didn't need feeding yet, and said nothing more that night. But something had shifted at that fire all the same — not healed, none of them were foolish enough to mistake it for healed, but witnessed, for the first time, by people who understood exactly what it cost her to let them.

Taya caught Toria's eye across the flame, the same way she had the morning Tiata asked where they were headed. Neither of them said anything either.

Some things, it turned out, only needed saying once.
