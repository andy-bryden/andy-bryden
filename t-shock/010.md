# 10 — A Silent Station

Taya found it two days before the others reached it — a timber watch-tower and a low palisade wall, set on a rise where the old frontier road bent north, manned by fewer men than even Tiata had guessed.

"Wood, not stone," she reported, back at the treeline where the others waited. "One gate. The watchtower at the northeast corner, one man in it most hours, sometimes none. They've gone soft here."

"Good," Tiata said. "Soft is what we came for."

This was not a patrol, strung out and vulnerable on open ground. A watch-station had walls and a gate, and men inside it who could bar a door and buy time even in their panic.

"No one escapes," Tiata said. "Not a rider, not a runner, not a shout carried far enough to matter. This isn't a message we're sending with a survivor. This is a message we're sending with silence. Rome doesn't get an account of what happened here. It gets an absence."

It was Taya who took the tower, going up and over the palisade in the dead hour before dawn. The man on watch never called out. He simply stopped being a threat, and the tower's height became theirs instead of his.

Thana and Tacita took the gate. Toria and Tiata went through the barracks door together, and what followed inside wasn't the kind of fighting any of them had done in open ground — no ambush to spring, no formation to shatter, just close, fast, brutal work in cramped quarters, men waking to their own deaths.

It was over before the sky had properly lightened. Nine men, and not one of them had gotten so much as a shout past the walls.

They left it standing — gate shut again, palisade intact, everything in its place except the men who should have been walking it. From a distance, riding past on the frontier road, it would look like nothing was wrong at all. That delay was the whole point. The longer it looked normal, the longer before anyone thought to ask why it had stopped answering.

They took two horses from the station's small paddock before they left — sturdy, unremarkable frontier stock. Toria claimed one, and Thana took the other, muttering something about finally not having to share a saddle with anyone's elbows.

"How long before someone notices?" Toria asked.

"Depends how badly Rome wants to admit it's stopped watching its own frontier," Tiata said. "Days, if we're fortunate. Longer, if the men meant to check on it are as soft as the men we just met." She looked out over the quiet palisade, the closed gate, the ordinary morning settling over the station. "Either way, by the time anyone works out what happened here..." she trailed off.

They struck north again into the deep timber, leaving the station standing silent behind them.
