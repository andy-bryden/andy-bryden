# 32 — Phenomenon

The party had ridden out at first light, and the Praefectus had stayed in the yard through the whole of the morning, until they returned.

Four rode in, and the rest walked beside litters, ten of them, with canvas drawn over them.

The Praefectus approached the nearest rider.

"Well?" he said. "Well?"

The rider was young and it took him a moment to understand he was the one being addressed.

"Sir, we recovered six more of the fallen, as well as the four fallen the previous evening. The ground was—"

"Is he among them."

"No, sir."

"Then where."

"We don't know, sir. We searched as far as—"

"You searched." He bellowed. "You searched and you come back and tell me you don't know."

"Sir, every man who rode out yesterday is accounted for now. Six dead. Fifty-four returned. Centurion Marcellus is the only one who isn't."

"Then go out again," he said. "Now. Every man who can still sit a horse."

"Sir, the light—"

"I did not ask you the hour." He had hold of the rider's arm now. "You will turn this province inside out before you tell me a man of mine isn't anywhere. Men don't simply—"

"Praefectus."

Centurion Rufinus had come up behind him, and touched his arm.

"Let him go, sir. He's told you what there is."

"There is more. There has to be more."

"There isn't. Not today. Not from him."

The yard had gone quiet. The litters were coming through. A horse shifted somewhere behind him and whinnied.

"Sir," Rufinus said. "Not here."

"I am not finished—"

"You are finished here."

Centurion Draco took the other arm, and between the two of them, they took him into the record room off the praetorium and shut the door.

It was several minutes before he was calm enough to speak rationally.

"They saw that," he said.

"They saw a man who's lost more in two days than most commanders lose in a year," Draco said. "It will pass."

"It will not pass. Four men. Then a woman gone from my own house without a trace. Then a full century sent out and come back in pieces. Now this. My cousin. Gone. Not found. Just not there."

Neither centurion had an answer to this.

"You need to send word up the line," Rufinus said. "Tonight. Before it's a week old and looks like something you sat on."

"And say what." He sounded exhausted. "Say that seven women ended a patrol and left a witness on purpose. Say that a woman kept three years under guard walked out of a secure fort leaving no trace. Say a centurion, my own cousin, vanished off a battlefield, right under the noses of his own century. Say that the same century returned in total disarray, having been shattered by the same hands - by seven women. Say that the entire cavalry division of that century returned on foot, having had - every man of them - his steed cut down beneath him. And I have no consistent account of any of it because not one man among them saw it clearly enought to give account." He looked at them both. "Tell me which part of that I write down, so whoever reads it doesn't think I've lost my mind."

"Write what happened," Draco said. "Not what it means. Numbers. Names. What was seen, and by how many. Let them draw their own conclusions."

"And if their conclusion is that I imagined it?"

"Then it's their error, not yours," Rufinus said. "But you don't get to decide that by staying silent instead."

The Praefectus sat a long while at the table without touching the wax. Twice he started a line and stopped, reading back what he'd written.

In the end, he wrote it all. Numbers. Names. What was seen, and by how many. He left out the word "phenomenon", though it was the only word that could rightly describe what was happening. Written down, addressed to a man who'd never stood in his yard, it didn't make any of it easier to believe.
