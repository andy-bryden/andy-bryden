# 28 — Captive Ignored
 
Tacita and Thana walked the centurion in, blade still at his throat, to the edge of the clearing where the firelight thinned into shadow — close enough to hear every word said around that fire, too far from it to feel any of its warmth.
 
They sat him against a lone birch just outside the light and bound his wrists behind it, then his ankles, and walked away, Thana casting a single sneer back in his direction before completely ignoring him.
 
Taran's eyes stayed on him a moment longer.
 
Tove had built the fire up higher than usual, feeding it with her usual generosity. Thilda sat close to the flame with her spear across her knees, unhurriedly working a nick out of the blade with a whetstone. Thana stretched her legs toward the fire and let her head fall back, eyes half-closed, as if she'd done nothing more strenuous that day than stroll to the stream.
 
"Four horses down before any of them understood what was happening," Toria said. "I've never seen a line come apart that fast."
 
"They never had a chance to hold anything," Taya said, not looking up from the arrow in her hands. "They split their own line chasing two shouts that weren't where the real danger was. By the time they went back for the horses, half the century was still in the trees looking the wrong way."
 
Tiata sat a little apart, the falx laid flat across her knees. She'd already heard the whole of it once, walking back — every angle, every kill, exactly how the ambush had folded itself back into the trees.
 
It was Thana who turned the talk toward Taran.
 
"And our newest terror," Thana said, tipping her chin at Taran, the old sharpness softened. "South side, drawing off half their foot before any of them knew there was a fight. First real work with us, and she puts an arrow through a man's throat before he's gone ten paces into the trees. I'd call that a fair morning's introduction."
 
"I had the angle Taya gave me," Taran said. "She read the ground. I only had to be where she said."
 
"You only had to hold and not flinch," Taya said. "Most people flinch the first time it's real. You didn't."
 
Tove reached over and knocked a shoulder against Taran, warm and solid. "First time with us, and already better than half the recruits I've ever seen a Roman drillmaster claim credit for."
 
"I've held a bow longer than I've held anything else," Taran said. She turned the yew stave over in her lap, the wood worn smooth in places. "This very one. Just not for three years."
 
Even Tiata leaned in at this.
 
"I went into the armory for the blades," Taran said. "I never knew what he'd done with my bow — never really thought about it."
 
She paused.
 
"It was hanging above his own armory rack. Something for his guests to admire and told about — the wild woman's bow, same as the wild woman herself." Her jaw set, hard, before she went on. "I never knew he'd kept it. Not until I saw it hanging there last noght."
 
She ran a thumb lovingly along the grain.
 
"I took it down with my own two hands." She said, with a hint of satisfaction. "It still remembers my draw."
 
Tove's hand found her shoulder again, no words, just presence.
 
"Then it's yours again." Tiata said, at last.
 
The fire crackled. Somewhere past its light and warmth, sat a man, with his wrists bound behind his back, and every word had reached his ears.
 
Thilda finished with the whetstone and laid the spear aside, testing the edge once against her thumb before she seemed satisfied. "First watch," she said, already rising. "I'll take it."
 
"I'll spell you at the turn," Taya said, and that was the evening finished.
