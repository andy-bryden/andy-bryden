# 13 — West

Nobody answered right away. It was Tacita, in the end, who did.

She rose from where she'd been sitting and drew her sword. She held it out, arm level, blade steady, and turned until it settled due west.

That was the whole of her answer. She didn't sheath the blade again right away, and she didn't look to Tiata, or to anyone, for confirmation.

Tove studied the line of the blade for a moment, then looked back at the fire, at all of them, with something that might have been the beginning of real respect. "West," she said. "Away from the Hasdingi. Away from the people who sold me."

"Away from what took you," Tiata said, "but not away from what it means. West is only a direction, not a destination."

They were moving before the fire had fully died, west and further west. Tove fell into the line easily.

It was Taya, again, who found the trouble before the trouble found them.

"Party ahead," she reported, dropping back from a rise just wide enough to see through the trees. "Six, maybe seven. Moving east — toward us. Loaded down, from the look of their pace. Coming home from somewhere."

Tove's jaw tightened at the word. "Hasdingi carry themselves a particular way when they're heavy with plunder," she said. "That's them."

"Then they're a long way from home to be careless," Tiata said. "Taya, the same as always. Tove—" she paused, "—this one's yours to lead, if you want to."

Tove's answer wasn't in words. She simply hefted the axe, already moving toward the treeline that would put her closest to whatever came first.

The Hasdingi party walked into the ambush carelessly — strung out, talking too loud. Taya's arrow took the first, and the line broke into chaos.

Tove didn't wait for the formation to fully collapse. She went in low and fast, her axe finding the gap between one man's shield and his ribs before he'd registered the threat. There was nothing showy in it, just a raw, focused violence that hadn't been visible at all around the fire where they'd found her, and Toria, working the opposite flank, found herself admiring the passion of their new companion.

Thana and Tacita closed the rest of it — fast, economical, no wasted motion — and within minutes the party that had walked out so carelessly wasn't walking anywhere again.

Tove stood over the last of them a moment longer, breathing hard, something unreadable in her eyes.

"Feel any different?" Toria asked, not unkindly.

"No," Tove said, after a moment. "I thought it might. It doesn't." She wiped the axe clean, matter-of-fact again, the warmth already creeping back into her voice. "Turns out revenge isn't really what I wanted."

They took what the party carried and pushed on until the light had nearly gone, making camp in a hollow Taya chose.

The fire that night burned easy. Tove told the story of the boar and even the corners of Tacita's mouth resembled a smile.
