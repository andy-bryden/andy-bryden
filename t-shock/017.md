# 17 — Away from the Frontier

They didn't break camp with the morning, and nobody suggested they should.

Tiata sat with the fire still smoking between them, the falx across her knees the way it always rested when she meant to think rather than move, and looked at Thilda with the same flat attention she gave any question worth actually answering.

"You know this ground better than any of us," she said. "Better than me, and I've spent longer reading frontiers than most. Before we decide where we're riding next, I'd rather hear where you'd take us."

It wasn't a small thing, and Thilda understood that plainly enough not to waste it. She'd spent a year listening to men who gave orders without ever once asking what the ground actually permitted, and the difference sat with her for a moment before she answered.

"North along the border, the way you've been moving, you'll keep finding what you've already found," she said. "Watch-posts, patrols, the occasional supply line worth bleeding. Useful. Steady. It won't ever be more than steady, though — Rome expects losses along that stretch, budgets for them, barely blinks." She nodded, slowly, westward, away from the frontier entirely. "West, the ground changes. Fewer eyes, less discipline, tribes still working out who they are and who they owe anything to. Harder country to move through safely. But anything you do there, nobody's expecting, and nobody Roman has to explain losing it — because officially, Rome isn't watching that ground closely enough to have lost anything there at all."

"An absence, rather than an account," Tiata said, echoing her own words from the watch-station without seeming to notice she'd done it.

"If that's how you put it, yes." Thilda's mouth twitched, not quite a smile. "I've spent a year learning exactly how much Rome doesn't watch what it doesn't think it needs to. It's more than you'd guess."

Taya, listening from the edge of the fire, spoke up without being asked, which was itself its own small signal of how far the morning had already shifted. "If we go west, I'll need days ahead of the rest of you to read it properly. Unfamiliar ground, unfamiliar people — I won't move us blind into either."

"Take what you need," Tiata said, without hesitation.

Tove leaned back, arms folded, something considering in her expression. "West also puts more distance between us and everyone who might still be looking east for us," she offered. "No harm in that, this far along."

"And less distance from whatever's waiting for us once we get there," Toria said — not an objection, simply the other half of the same coin, laid out plainly the way she always laid things out.

Tiata let all of it settle before she spoke again, the same unhurried weighing she gave everything, each voice folded into the decision rather than argued past.

"West, then," she said, at last. "Not blindly. Taya goes ahead alone, same as always — she'll move faster and quieter without the rest of us slowing her. The rest of us hold somewhere safe until she's read enough of it to bring us in behind her."

Nobody argued. By now, none of them expected to need to — not because Tiata's word was law, but because everyone at that fire had just watched her actually listen before she gave it.

"There's a hollow half a day south of here," Thilda said, into the pause that followed. "Good water, better cover, nothing on the approach that isn't visible a long way off. If Taya needs days to read what's west of us properly, that's where I'd sit and wait for her word, rather than push the rest of us blind into ground even she hasn't seen yet."

Taya nodded once, already turning the shape of it over. "That works. I'll move faster alone than any of you could keep pace with regardless."

"Then that's settled," Tiata said.

They broke camp within the hour — not west yet, but south, toward the hollow Thilda had named, seven of them moving together while Taya struck out alone ahead, into ground none of the rest of them had seen before.
