 Author's Note.
 Fort Lorkh was a real Roman garrison. It sat at a point known today as the Leemess-ki'knee — literally, the "leemess-knee," the bend where the Upper Germanic leemess turned sharply east to meet the Ree-shen leemess on its way to the Danube. Gheenoo is the Latin word for knee, and it's the word Taran uses for it in this book.
 The site lies beneath the present-day town of Lorkh, which has prevented the same kind of full excavation carried out at other forts along the frontier, such as tszook-marn-tel and the Leemess Gate at Dalkingen. The only part of the fort still visible above ground is a single tower base at the west gate. The last significant excavation was carried out in the 1980s, and found the remains preserved under about 1.5 metres of hillside silt and more recent construction.
 The wider collapse of the frontier referenced in this book is also historical, known as the Leemessfahl. The worst of it came later than this book's setting, mainly in the 250s AD, but pressure from the Alemahni had already begun by 233. Several forts near Lorkh, including the Leemess Gate at Dalkingen, were burned and abandoned around this same period.
