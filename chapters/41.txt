Chapter 41. — Every Side.
 Lig-arius had sent his report north that afternoon, sealed and given to a courier bound for Mohgoon-tee-ar-koom. He meant to follow within the week. His part at Lorkh was done, and he had told the pryfektus so over the evening meal.
 The pryfektus had ordered the best wine he'd yet put in front of him. The talk ran easier than it had since Lig-arius arrived — old postings, a mutual acquaintance at Mohgoon-tee-ar-koom, nothing that touched the western ground.
 Suddenly, shouts rose from every side — north, east, south.
 The pryfektus was on his feet and moving toward the yard with Lig-arius close behind him, out through the prytorium and into the evening air.
 Men were running toward the north rampart stair. Others were running toward the south. A knot of soldiers stood crowding around the eastern gate, and a runner was crossing the open ground at a sprint, heading directly for the pryfektus.
 "Sir." The runner reached them out of breath. "North wall. A sentry's down. Arrow. From the north-east ridge."
 A second man arrived before the first had finished speaking. "South wall the same, sir. Both of them, struck at the same moment."
 The pryfektus was visibly shaken. He turned toward the gate, where the knot of soldiers had begun to move, and Lig-arius went with him.
 They parted to let the pryfektus through. In the middle of them stood a soldier stripped to his under-tunic, skin raised in gooseflesh in the night chill.
 The pryfektus had seen this before. It was the same pattern as the first strike, several weeks earlier.
 "Report," the pryfektus said.
 "Five of us went out on the eastern loop." The soldier's voice was halting. "We didn't come back on time. They were waiting for us on the bend before the ford. I'm the one they sent back with a message."
 "How many," the pryfektus said.
 "I never saw more than four. Could have been more I didn't see."
 "Weapons."
 "I don't know, sir. It was fast."
 Lig-arius stepped in before the pryfektus could ask the next question, and the pryfektus let him.
 "You said they sent you back with a message," Lig-arius said. "What was the message?"
 "The big one said to tell your visitor we look forward to meeting him."
 Lig-arius paused at this. "Is that all they said?"
 "No, sir," said the soldier. "They said 'We haven't gone anywhere, have we?'"
 "And the north wall," Lig-arius said. "The south. Did they say anything of those."
 "No. Nothing."
 Lig-arius looked first to the north stair, then to the south, then to the gate where the soldier stood. He said nothing for a moment.
 "Bring me the men who saw the wall shots," Lig-arius said. "Whoever was nearest each one. I want them separately, and I want them now."
 The order went out through Rufeenus. Within a quarter hour Lig-arius had two men in front of him, in the record room off the prytorium, the pryfektus standing by while Lig-arius asked the questions.
 Both accounts agreed on the key points. Neither man had seen anything at the tree line, or heard a sound before the sentry beside him fell. Both had the timing to within moments of each other. Neither could say from what distance the shot had come, only that it came from deep within in the tree line.
 Lig-arius set down his stylus and closed his eyes for a moment before he spoke again.
 "Two dead on the wall," he said. "Struck within the same breath, on two sides of this fort that face away from each other. A patrol taken on a third side, the same evening, by the same method that was used on the first attack. One survivor released, stripped, sent back walking." He looked up at the pryfektus. "I've spent the last two days telling myself this was contained to the ground west your walls. I don't know what to think now."
 "Then it's not seven women in a forest," the pryfektus said. "It's something that can stand at my own walls and put arrows through my sentries."
 "It's still seven women," Lig-arius said. "Or eight. I haven't changed my count. What's changed is where I believed the edge of their reach to be." He stood. "I know where their camp is. I've stood in it myself. And by every report I've gathered since I arrived, it hasn't moved in over two weeks."
 "You think that carelessness."
 "I think it confidence, possibly overconfidence," Lig-arius said. "This garrison has refused to set foot on that ground since well before I arrived. They've had two proofs now that nobody from this fort will follow them home. A band that bold, sitting that long in one place, isn't hiding from you. It's decided it doesn't need to." He paused. "Tonight, while every man in this yard is looking at his own dead, that camp is very likely unwatched. They've had no reason yet to think we'd dare attack."
 The pryfektus looked at him for a long moment.
 "You're proposing we go to them."
 "Before sunrise tomorrow," Lig-arius said.
