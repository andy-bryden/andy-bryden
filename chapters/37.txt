Chapter 37. — The Rider.
 Tigratavaah had watched the fort from the same ridge for eleven days, taking the dawn hours because that was when a gate crew was most likely to be tired and careless. Most of what she'd seen in that time hadn't changed: a sentry walking the rampart, smoke starting from the kitchen fires, no patrol willing to cross the western ground. This morning a rider came down the road from the northwest.
 He rode alone. A packhorse followed him on a lead, loaded with two hide cases and a bedroll. No escort came with him, and his pace didn't match a courier's — couriers rode fast and pushed their mounts hard; this man came on at a walk.
 His horse was better fed and better shod than anything she'd seen a common soldier ride. He wore a plain travelling cloak, no armour visible under it, and carried a leather document case slung across his back.
 The gate opened for him before he reached it. She had watched that gate for weeks. Every other arrival — patrol, courier, muster — had been made to stop and give a name before the gate moved. This one wasn't stopped at all.
 She went back and told Tiata what she'd seen: one rider, one packhorse, a document case, no escort, admitted without being stopped.
 "Was he questioned at the gate," Tiata asked.
 "No. It opened for him before he'd reached it."
 "Any second rider. Held back out of sight."
 "None that I saw."
 "How was he dressed."
 "A travelling cloak. No armour showing under it. He carried a case for documents on his back."
 Tiata sat with this for a while before she spoke again, the falx flat across her knees.
 "A courier gets stopped at that gate the same as anyone else," she said. "Every man who's come down that road since we've been watching it has been made to give his name first. This one wasn't. That means someone at Lorkh already knew he was coming, and had told the gate to let him straight through."
 "Either that, or they recognised him," To-riaah said. "Perhaps he's an officer sent from another fort."
 "An officer travels with at least one man at his back, usually more," Tiata said. "This one came alone, with nothing but supplies and papers. That rules out an officer. It could still be a man the gate crew knows by sight — but a familiar face doesn't explain the case of documents, or why he came alone instead of with whatever business usually brings him to Lorkh. I'd rather read him as someone sent to look at what happened here and write it down — someone who answers to an office above the pryfektus, not under him."
 "So he's here to ask questions," Thilda said.
 "That's my reading of it," Tiata said. "And if he's been sent from outside Lorkh's own chain, it means whatever the pryfektus already reported was thought serious enough for someone further up to check it in person, rather than take his word for it."
 To-riaah said nothing for a moment. "Tigratavaah, you said something like this yourself, the night after we let the pryfektus find Markellus. That a frightened system doesn't only lose its nerve. Sometimes it looks harder instead."
 "That certainly seems to be what's happening," Tigratavaah said.
 "This is what that looks like," Tiata said. "It's also what we wanted. To-riaah, I recall you saying yourself, the first morning I rode with any of you — not to beat Rome in open ground, but to make the men running it start doubting the ground they're standing on. A man like this doesn't get sent unless someone above the pryfektus already doubts him. That's exactly the doubt we set out to cause. It doesn't mean we've won." She paused, then added, "Yet."
 She looked slowly around the group before continuing. "It simply means the stakes are higher, and that's exactly what we want."
