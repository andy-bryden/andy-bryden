Chapter 42. — Torches.
 Tovv-eh had the last watch before dawn the following morning, and it was still over an hour till first light when the torches in the fort began to be lit.
 The fort usually held no torches through the night, not since the first week Tigratavaah had watched it. Tonight the wall was lit end to end, and men were moving in the yard.
 She went back to alert Tiata.
 "Something's happening at the fort," she said. "Torches lit along the whole wall. The yard is alive with men on the move."
 Tiata was on her feet without a word, and the rest followed, watching in silence as a column formed in the pre-dawn dark — foot and horse both, more men than either recovery party had mustered.
 They watched the column pass through the gate and take its bearing.
 "They're headed for the old site," Tahna said.
 "They are indeed," Tiata said.
 She turned to Tovv-eh. "You had us move camp yesterday. What did you see that I didn't?"
 "I really don't know," Tovv-eh paused. "I just didn't like how quiet it had been."
 "Keep not liking it," Tiata said. "It's useful."
 To-riaah had been watching the gate rather than the departing column. "I reckon more than half that garrison just walked out its own front door," she said. "That's an opportunity we shouldn't miss."
 "We can't take the fort," Taran said.
 "Nobody's suggesting that," said To-riaah. "But we can give them a surprise to come home to."
 They talked through it standing where they were — the gate, the granary, the stables. It was Tigratavaah who finally had an idea that might work.
 "There are two ridges to the north that look straight down into the yard," she said. "I've marked them before. Those torches they've lit for the muster — I can put an arrow into more than one from up there. If enough come down in the right place, that yard is going to catch fire."
 "You're certain of the range," Tiata said.
 "I am."
 "Of course you are," Tiata said. "I needn't have asked."
 "No harm in certainty," said Tigratavaah.
 She turned to Taran. "I reckon you could knock some torches, too."
 "Gladly," said Taran, as she began to collect her gear.
 They went east first, keeping the fort's bulk between themselves and the column, then cut north and climbed the ridge. From their vantage point, the yard sat open beneath them, torches burning along the wall and a scatter more further in, near the stores.
 Tigratavaah took the first. The torch left the bracket and rolled, still burning, into what looked like stacked fodder. Taran matched her a moment later, and then again, until five had come down. One went out on the packed earth, but the others found fuel.
 They made their way around to the north-west ridge, and once again, the yard opened out beneath them.
 Three more torches found a resting place among flammable material, and it wasn't long before the stables began to burn.
 They found a rise close enough to watch both the fort and the road the column had taken, and waited.
 The fire took hold slowly, at first. Figures ran along the wall with buckets, but the flames were soon showing over the rampart.
 The column came back into view shortly after sunrise, all semblance of order gone.
