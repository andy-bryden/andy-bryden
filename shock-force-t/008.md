# 8 — The Cost of Silence

They returned to Tiata's camp, and Tacita built the fire, quickly and without wasted motion. She had always been the quietest of them, and by now the others had simply accepted her as she was, but Tiata noticed it differently.

"You haven't spoken since I joined you," she said, once the fire had caught and they had all settled. It was a flat, exact statement, and somehow more disarming than sympathy would have been.

Tacita didn't look up from the fire.

"You either don't speak because you can't, or because you've decided against it." Tiata said, "You're not the first kind."

Tacita still remained silent, but her shoulders had begun to relax.

Thana shifted, about to step in, but Tiata lifted one hand, and Thana settled again.

"I don't ask to be cruel," Tiata said. "I ask because I've noticed you're the one among all of us who never once has to be corrected. Taya reads people. Thana reads fights. Toria acts before she thinks and is usually right anyway. But you—" she paused, choosing her words carefully, "—you never do anything that needs undoing. That's not nature. That's discipline. And discipline like that is built, not born, whatever quiet you started with."

The fire crackled, and one of the horses whinnied.

After another long silence, Tacita spoke. "I was always quiet."

The words came out low. It was the first full sentence any of them had heard from her.

Tiata simply nodded. "I imagine you were. But quiet isn't silence. Not this kind." She paused a moment before going on, more gently. "What made the difference?"

Tacita was quiet again for a long while. She was still looking at the fire when she finally spoke again, each word costing her great effort.

"My father," she said. "Owed a debt he could not pay." She paused. "He found a way to pay it that did not cost him anything he valued."

"Rome bought what he sold," she said. "That is all there is."

Tiata simply nodded.

"That's enough," she said, a note of respect creeping into her voice.

Tacita went back to the fire, but a stillness stole over the little group. They had all witnessed a change in Tacita. She had opened up in a way she had never done before, and only those who understood her could appreciate what it had cost her.
