# 12 — Rugii

The boar was good, and Tove made sure everyone said so before she let the conversation move on.

She fed the fire generously, yet without waste, tearing off the better cuts of meat and passing them along before anyone asked for more, laughing easily at Thana's dry remarks in a way that made even Thana's smirk soften into something like genuine amusement. By the time the meal was down to bones and embers, she'd made herself the most immediately likable person any of them had met.

It was Tiata who finally set the warmth aside long enough to ask what the rest of them had been too well-mannered to ask outright.

"You're not Gothic," she said. "You're not local to any tribe within a hundred miles of this fire. And you speak Latin better than most of the Romans I've killed for the privilege of practicing mine." She said it with the same flat directness she always used. "I'd like to know how you came to be sitting alone in this forest, that far from wherever you started."

Tove didn't flinch. If anything she seemed almost relieved to finally have the question asked.

"Rugii," she said. "Though I doubt the name means much to any of you this far south. My people held the coast a long way north of here — lovely cold water, bitterly cold winters. The kind of place Rome's never once bothered sending a legion, because there's nothing there Rome wants badly enough to freeze for it." A faint, wry smile touched the corner of her mouth. "The Goths wanted it badly enough, though."

"Gothic raiders took you," Tiata said.

"Took quite a few of us, over a few bad years. I wasn't the only one, and I wouldn't have been the last." Tove turned a piece of bone over in her hands, not quite looking at any of them now. "They didn't keep me for themselves, not for long. I was traded on. Somewhere in all that moving, I ended up in the keeping of Vandal traders. Hasdingi, if the distinction matters to you."

"It does," Tiata said, quietly.

"Then you'll know they'd made themselves useful to Rome by then. Land for loyalty, or something close enough to it — I never heard the whole of the arrangement, only enough to understand that whatever I was worth to them, some part of that worth was owed, one way or another, to keeping Rome content." The wryness in her voice had hardened into a touch of bitterness.

"How did you get free of them?" Toria asked.

"Badly, and slowly, and mostly by being underestimated," Tove said, the warmth creeping back into her voice, threaded now with real satisfaction. "People who move captives for profit get careless about which of us they think capable of anything beyond carrying what we're told to carry. I waited until careless turned into an actual mistake, and then I made sure it cost them more than they'd bargained for." She shrugged, as if the details weren't worth the breath. "I've been on my own since. Long enough now that I'd stopped expecting company worth keeping — until an hour of watching five strangers not quite manage to sneak up on me."

That drew something close to a real laugh around the fire, Taya included, which was itself no small thing.

"Any interest in trading one kind of alone for a different kind?" Toria asked, when the laughter had settled.

Tove looked at each of them in turn.

"I've been waiting for something worth my time since I stopped being anyone's captive and started being nobody's anything," she said. "I don't know yet if that's you. But I know I'd rather find out than spend another season alone in this forest wondering." She picked her axe back up, resting it easy across her knees the way Tiata rested her falx. "So. Where are we headed?"
