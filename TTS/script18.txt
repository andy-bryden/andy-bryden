 Chapter 18. — The Fort.

 Tig'rah-ta'vaa left before the others had finished breaking camp. She took the mare and nothing that could rattle or catch light.
 She passed Alemaani farmsteads first, scattered and new, the wood still pale and unweathered. Further on the land roughened into forest. She slowed the mare to a walk and read the ground at that pace instead.
 She found a trail by midmorning: old but used, running south, worn by feet rather than hooves. People without horses to spare. Poorer country, and likely the loose, unwatched ground Thilda had promised.
 It wasn't long before the trail crossed a ford — shallow and worn smooth by long use, with nothing standing guard over it. No palisade. No watchtower. Every Roman crossing she'd ever scouted had one, even a poorly manned one. This had none. She sat above it a while, working out what Thilda had meant back at the fire — not ground Rome had forgotten, ground Rome had never bothered learning about in the first place.
 That alone would have been enough for the day, but she followed the far bank west a while instead, out of habit, and that was when she saw it — straight lines against the tree line, where nothing straight grew on its own.
 The fort. Stone footings, from what she could make out, and enough of a footprint to house a real garrison. She spent the rest of the afternoon working the tree line in a slow half-circle, getting close enough to be sure without being seen.
 It sat at the exact point where the frontier turned north and stopped watching west, just as Thilda had said. The walls faced east and north, back the way the frontier came from. On the west side, behind the fort itself, a supply track ran wide and open — wagons coming and going on a plain schedule, because nobody expected trouble from that direction.
 She counted what she could see. Enough men to matter, not a skeleton crew. That supply road was undefended, and the whole fort was built facing away from the road.
 This was the target To'ria had imagined, back before any of this had a plan behind it — a fixed point, not a scratch Rome could shrug off. Nobody had known then where to find one. Now there was one, sitting exposed on its blind side.
 She marked the road, the blind approach, and the two places a rider could cross the ford unseen, then turned back before the light went.
 It was full dusk by the time she reached the hollow, later than she'd said she'd be. Tee'aahta was on her feet before Tig'rah-ta'vaa had cleared the tree line.
 "You found it," Tee'aahta said.
 "I found the garrison," Tig'rah-ta'vaa said.
 The others gathered without being called. Tig'rah-ta'vaa laid it out plainly — the ford, the fort, the supply road nobody was watching.
 "Stone-built. Real numbers, not a skeleton crew. We're not walking in and clearing it the way we cleared the watch-station." She looked at To'ria. "But it's the one you asked for. Back before Tee'aahta, before any of this had a plan. That road is wide open. We can't take the fort, but we can make the men inside it stop trusting it."
 To'ria was quiet a moment. "How long to plan it properly?"
 "Longer than tonight," Tig'rah-ta'vaa said. "But it can be done. I saw how."
 Tee'aahta looked around the fire once — To'ria still, Tahna already turning it over, Tasset'uh watching, Tov-aeh's hand resting on the axe across her knees, Thilda leaning in.
 "Then that's where we're going," Tee'aahta said.
