 Chapter 39. — Watched in Turn.

 Thilda held the ridge above the fort's east gate, the morning after the outsider had arrived.
 It was about mid-morning when the escort came out, four men on foot, one centurion and the rider Tig'rah-ta'vaa had seen the previous morning. They were keeping a steady walking pace, and were heading directly for the ambush site.
 She was back at camp within the half hour.
 "There's an escort heading this way," she said. "Four foot soldiers, one centurion and the stranger."
 Tee'aahta was already on her feet. "Not a search."
 "Not a search," Thilda said. "They are headed directly for the ambush site."
 They left the camp standing and moved to the ground above the clearing, keeping to the tree line the whole way. Below them, Ligahrius worked the churned earth on foot, crouching at the drag marks, following them out to where the recovery carts had stood. He worked the site methodically, noting all his findings on a wax tablet he kept in his document pouch.
 "He's thorough," Tig'rah-ta'vaa said.
 "None of the other five have spoken to him since they left the gate," Tee'aahta said.
 When Ligahrius straightened and turned to the escort, asking them to take him to the camp, the band followed, staying out of earshot.
 The escort's nerve broke within sight of the camp.
 Rufeenus appeared to be ordering the four soldiers to continue on, but none of them moved. Ligahrius waited, saying nothing, while the argument played out in front of him. In the end they watched Ligahrius continue alone.
 Tee'aahta turned to alert Tig'rah-ta'vaa to prepare her bow, and found her with bow half-drawn, and arrow tracking Ligahrius across the open ground. Tee'aahta nodded and said nothing.
 He stood at the edge of the clearing a while before he moved further in, taking in the fire, the horses, the bedrolls, without touching any of it. He crouched by the fire once, close enough to feel its heat, and didn't disturb the ash. They watched him counting the bedrolls, moving his lips.
 Tig'rah-ta'vaa's bow didn't waver the whole time.
 He left the way he'd come, unhurried, and joined the escort to return to the fort.
 "The eight bedrolls seemed to interest him," Tov-aeh said, with a chuckle in her voice.
 "That's it," Tahna said. "The boy from the first patrol would have reported seven, and nobody in that second attack would have been able to count anything."
 Tahran's jaw was tight. "He'll figure out I'm here, won't he?"
 "You've left Lorkh, that much he knows," Tee'aahta said. "But for him to believe that you're still this close, he'll be thinking either he's mad, or you are." She held Tahran's gaze a moment. "Rome won't attack what it doesn't believe in."
