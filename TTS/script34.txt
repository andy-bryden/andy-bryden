 Chapter 34. — Rome on Its Head.

 The last frightened Roman was barely out of earshot before the clearing rang with laughter, and those eight pairs of merciless eyes finally let the amusement show.
 "I keep thinking about the gate," Tov-aeh said, turning a piece of kindling in her hands without feeding it to the fire yet. "Not tonight's gate. Tomorrow's. The next watchman who has to stand it, knowing what stood outside it last week and walked away without anyone catching a glimpse."
 "He won't sleep," Tahna said. "None of them will. We didn't have to kill the whole garrison to ruin their sleep for the season — we only had to send one man back to remind the rest what waiting for us feels like." She stretched toward the fire. "I've fought plenty who feared dying. This is the first time I've built one that fears living."
 Tig'rah-ta'vaa nodded, not looking up from the arrow she was fletching. "A man afraid of dying is done costing you anything the moment he's dead. A man afraid of living carries it into every room he sits in, for as long as he lasts. That's worth more to us than half a garrison dead."
 Tahna laughed. "I won't argue that."
 To'ria had been quiet longer than usual. "I keep thinking about tonight's report," she said. "The one the Pryfectus has to write now, once his hand stops shaking enough to hold the stylus." She looked into the fire rather than at any of them. "What does a man like that even say. I lost a patrol. I lost a woman I kept three years and never once thought could leave. I lost a century, and my own cousin's mind besides, and none of it to an enemy I can name to a superior without sounding mad. I'd almost pity the wax."
 They all laughed.
 "It won't stay at Lorkh," Thilda said. "Whatever he writes goes up the line to someone who's never smelled that fort's air. That man reads numbers he can't reconcile, and he has two choices. Believe it, and admit the frontier's rotting. Or doubt it, and hope the next report proves him right." She fed the fire. "Either way, he won't sleep well either. Fear moves up a chain slower than it moves down one. But it does move up."
 "That's the part I like best," Tov-aeh said, leaning forward. "Not just Lorkh. Whoever sits above the man who sits above the Pryfectus. Some official who's never thought about a frontier fort by name, holding a dispatch that says eight women undid a century and walked a Roman officer home in a dress, and nobody can say how it was done." She shook her head. "That's not a bad week for one Pryfectus anymore. That's the whole of Rome on its head."
 "You're imagining a very large collapse from one very small dress," Tahna said.
 "Am I? Rome's whole trick is convincing everyone that who stands over whom can never change. Man over woman. Captor over captive. Citizen over everyone who isn't." Tov-aeh nodded toward Tahran. "Dress one man exactly the way he'd have dressed one of us, tether him exactly the way he tethered her, and you haven't just humiliated a centurion. You've shown every man who believed in that order that it isn't fixed. It only looked fixed because nobody had yet had the nerve to turn it around on them."
 Tahna let her head fall back against the log and gave a dry snort. "Maybe not on its head just yet," she said, the old smirk returning. "But we've got it on its knees, at least."
 Thilda looked up from testing the edge of her spear, glancing south through the dark toward where Lorkh lay hidden behind the ridges. "Right at the knee of their own frontier," she said.
 She turned to Tahran. "You probably heard them talking about the knee, did you?"
 "That's exactly what they called it," Tahran said. "Genu. The Knee. The officers cursed it every muster. Said a wall with a crook in its leg was twice the trouble to guard."
 Tahna let out a short laugh. "They were too busy watching the knee to see us coming up behind the hamstring."
 Tahran was sitting apart from the fire's full light, the bow across her knees, thumb moving along the grain.
 "I only care what it's done to a brute who used to hand me a practice blade and call it generous," she said, finally, eyes narrowing. "Three years, he stood there and let his cousin show me off like a hound that had learned to heel. Now half a garrison lies awake wondering if the thing they can't see is standing at their own door." She smiled a thin smile with no warmth in it. "Let the empire crack if it wants to. I only wanted the one house to know it isn't as safe as it thought."
 Tov-aeh's hand found her shoulder, but no further words were exchanged.
 Tig'rah-ta'vaa let the quiet hold a moment. "I'd be careful how satisfied we let ourselves get, all the same. A frightened system doesn't only despair. Sometimes it hunts harder, because it can't afford to be seen doubting itself. The bigger the crack we imagine we've made, the more men Rome will decide it can spend proving the crack isn't there." She set the finished arrow down. "Tonight, we've earned the fire. Tomorrow, we should assume they're already deciding how much they're willing to lose finding out we exist."
 "They already know we exist," To'ria said.
 "They know a story exists," Tig'rah-ta'vaa said. "There's a difference, and it's the difference that's kept us alive this long. Let's not spend it early because it felt good to imagine the size of what we'd done."
 Tee'aahta had let the whole of it run without steering it. Only now did she weigh in.
 "All of that is true, and exactly how we meant it to be," Tee'aahta said. She let the falx settle across her knees. "But Rome doesn't come apart because of one dead century, or one broken man, or one report a Pryfectus can't bring himself to write plainly. It comes apart from years of it, done over and over, by people it never thought worth watching. Tonight was one weight added to the pile. There'll be others before we're finished." She looked around the fire. "To'ria said as much, the first morning I rode with any of you. Not to beat Rome in open ground. To make the men running it start doubting the ground they're already standing on."
 Tasset'uh hadn't spoken. She fed the fire, reached over once and set her hand against Tahran's arm, and went back to the fire.
