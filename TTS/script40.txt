 Chapter 40. — Three Sides.

 "I think it's time we paid our new friend a visit," Tov-aeh said. "He came all this way. Be rude not to return the courtesy."
 "Gatecrashing seems impolite," To'ria said. "We should send word ahead. Let them know we're coming."
 "We could have sent it with those courier boys who just left," Tahna said.
 Tov-aeh grinned at that. "I must say, it would be nice to have a warm welcome when we visit."
 Tahran had been quiet, working a whetstone along one blade. She looked up.
 "Tig'rah-ta'vaa and I could send messages from both north and south," she said. "The same as we did with the century."
 Thilda turned that over for a moment. "That would work. Better still if we follow it with a patrol strike." She looked at Tee'aahta. "What do you say, Tee'aahta?"
 "I think we could make it work," Tee'aahta said. She turned to Tasset'uh. "Where would you put the strike?"
 Tasset'uh lifted her gladius and pointed it due east.
 "I like that idea," Tee'aahta said. "A patrol strike on the east would really upset the balance if we accompanied it with the shots on either flank. We'll want to get the timing right, though."
 "There's an eastern patrol that usually returns to the fort just on sundown," Thilda said. "I've already got a good ambush site in mind."
 "I reckon I know a couple of good vantage points, north and south, where the sentries on the ramparts are easily within striking distance," Tig'rah-ta'vaa said. "The wind around sundown is usually from the north-west, so the north shot will probably be the easier of the two. Do you have any preference, Tahran?"
 Tahran thought for a moment. "I'd like to try the south shot."
 "OK," Tee'aahta said, "sounds like you two have that part under control."
 She looked around the group. "The plan will be: strike the patrol, release a witness, split north and south." She paused. "We need to synchronise these two shots, so I'm thinking Tasset'uh could tail the witness to the fort, then signal the two of you the moment he enters the eastern gate."
 Tasset'uh looked across at Tee'aahta. She nodded once, and the corner of her mouth twitched.
 Tov-aeh had been quiet through the planning, turning something over. "There's a second part to this, if you want it," she said. "They've been in this camp twice now. If we accompany tonight's strike with a camp shift, they won't know where to look for us next."
 Tee'aahta considered that. "You might be onto something there. Any suggestions of where to go?"
 "Well, yes actually," Tov-aeh said. "There's a hollow to the south of the fort with good water, thick cover, and a rise behind it giving a clear line down onto the fort below."
 "I think I know the spot you mean," Tahran said. "I was looking at it yesterday."
 Tee'aahta stood up. "We'd better be packing up, then."

 They broke camp within the hour. Tov-aeh led them south, to the new camp site. It was mid-afternoon by the time the last of the gear was settled and the horses picketed.
 From there Thilda led them east, toward the ground she'd already walked twice. They reached it with the light beginning to slant low through the trees, and took the positions she'd chosen.
 The patrol came round the bend to find their path blocked. Thilda's shield filled half the width of the track, Tahna's oval shield locking in beside it. The patrol paused before raising their own shields.
 Tahran dropped from the branches overhead with a scream that even caught Tee'aahta off guard. She landed in the middle of their formation with her twin blades flying. Tov-aeh came in low on one flank, axe swinging, and To'ria appeared on the other side, falcata leveled.
 One man at the rear suddenly felt cold steel at his throat and a harsh rasp in his ear. "Stay."
 Tee'aahta and Tig'rah-ta'vaa stepped up and relieved him of his blade and shield, and began to strip him of his armour, leaving him in only his under-tunic.
 "We haven't gone anywhere, have we?" said Tee'aahta. "Off you go now, and tell your visitor we look forward to meeting him. Walk, don't run, and if you value your life, don't look behind you. Now, go."
 She turned him to face the fort and let him go.
 He began walking immediately, and Tasset'uh silently fell in behind him.
 To'ria's forearm had taken a blade, deeper than she'd first thought. Tov-aeh noticed it first, and immediately attended to her.
 "You'll live," Tov-aeh said, already binding the arm. "But you're done for today."
 Tov-aeh and Tahna walked with her back toward the new camp, To'ria leaning on Tov-aeh's shoulder.

 The rest split without discussion. Tee'aahta went north with Tig'rah-ta'vaa. Thilda went south with Tahran. Both pairs had marked their ground already, and both had a clear line to the same point east, where the returning soldier would have to pass on his way home.
 Tasset'uh went alone, following the young man's track at a distance.
 She watched him all the way to the gate. The moment he was admitted, she raised the gladius and held it there until she saw the response from both directions.

 Tahran had the sentry on the southern rampart in sight before the signal came. She loosed her arrow the moment she saw Tasset'uh raise her gladius, and the man folded without a sound.
 To the north, Tig'rah-ta'vaa's arrow found its mark in the same breath.
 Both women were already moving before either man had collapsed, gone before a shout could be raised on either wall.

 They came into the new camp after dark, Tasset'uh first, followed by the other two pairs. To'ria sat close to the fire, her arm bound and resting across her lap, watching the others settle.
 "Four on the road," Tig'rah-ta'vaa said, dropping to sit. "One north, one south. All in less time than it takes to walk a survivor through a gate."
 "He'll have barely finished passing on our regards," Tahna said.
 Tee'aahta looked around the fire at all of them, and let the quiet hold before she spoke.
 "I wonder how soon their visitor will be leaving," she said. "There isn't a side of that fort he can trust."
