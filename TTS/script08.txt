 Chapter 8. — The Cost of Silence.

 They returned to Tee'aahta's camp, and Tasset'uh built the fire, quickly and without wasted motion. She had always been the quietest of them, and by now the others had simply accepted her as she was, but Tee'aahta noticed it differently.
 "You haven't spoken since I joined you," she said, once the fire had caught and they had all settled. It was a flat, exact statement, and somehow more disarming than sympathy would have been.
 Tasset'uh didn't look up from the fire.
 "You either don't speak because you can't, or because you've decided against it," Tee'aahta said, "You're not the first kind."
 Tasset'uh still remained silent, but her shoulders had begun to relax.
 Tahna shifted, about to step in, but Tee'aahta lifted one hand, and Tahna settled again.
 "I don't ask to be cruel," Tee'aahta said. "I ask because I've noticed you're the one among all of us who never once has to be corrected. Tig'rah-ta'vaa reads people. Tahna reads fights. To'ria acts before she thinks and is usually right anyway. But you—" she paused, choosing her words carefully, "—you never do anything that needs undoing. That's not nature. That's discipline. And discipline like that is built, not born, whatever quiet you started with."
 The fire crackled, and one of the horses whinnied.
 After another long silence, Tasset'uh spoke. "I was always quiet."
 The words came out low. It was the first full sentence any of them had heard from her.
 Tee'aahta simply nodded. "I imagine you were. But quiet isn't silence. Not this kind." She paused a moment before going on, more gently. "What made the difference?"
 Tasset'uh was quiet again for a long while. She was still looking at the fire when she finally spoke again, each word costing her great effort.
 "My father," she said. "Owed a debt he could not pay." She paused. "He found a way to pay it that did not cost him anything he valued."
 "Rome bought what he sold," she said. "That is all there is."
 Tee'aahta simply nodded.
 "That's enough," she said, a note of respect creeping into her voice.
 Tasset'uh went back to the fire, but a stillness stole over the little group. They had all witnessed a change in Tasset'uh. She had opened up in a way she had never done before, and only those who understood her could appreciate what it had cost her.
