 Chapter 41. — Every Side.

 Ligahrius had sent his report north that afternoon, sealed and given to a courier bound for Mogoon-tee-aahkoom. He meant to follow within the week. His part at Lorkh was done, and he had told the Pryfectus so over the evening meal.
 The Pryfectus had ordered the best wine he'd yet put in front of him. The talk ran easier than it had since Ligahrius arrived — old postings, a mutual acquaintance at Mogoon-tee-aahkoom, nothing that touched the western ground.
 Suddenly, shouts rose from every side — north, east, south.
 The Pryfectus was on his feet and moving toward the yard with Ligahrius close behind him, out through the praetorium and into the evening air.
 Men were running toward the north rampart stair. Others were running toward the south. A knot of soldiers stood crowding around the eastern gate, and a runner was crossing the open ground at a sprint, heading directly for the Pryfectus.
 "Sir." The runner reached them out of breath. "North wall. A sentry's down. Arrow. From the north-east ridge."
 A second man arrived before the first had finished speaking. "South wall the same, sir. Both of them, struck at the same moment."
 The Pryfectus was visibly shaken. He turned toward the gate, where the knot of soldiers had begun to move, and Ligahrius went with him.
 They parted to let the Pryfectus through. In the middle of them stood a soldier stripped to his under-tunic, skin raised in gooseflesh in the night chill.
 The Pryfectus had seen this before. It was the same pattern as the first strike, several weeks earlier.
 "Report," the Pryfectus said.
 "Five of us went out on the eastern loop." The soldier's voice was halting. "We didn't come back on time. They were waiting for us on the bend before the ford. I'm the one they sent back with a message."
 "How many," the Pryfectus said.
 "I never saw more than four. Could have been more I didn't see."
 "Weapons."
 "I don't know, sir. It was fast."
 Ligahrius stepped in before the Pryfectus could ask the next question, and the Pryfectus let him.
 "You said they sent you back with a message," Ligahrius said. "What was the message?"
 "The big one said to tell your visitor we look forward to meeting him."
 Ligahrius paused at this. "Is that all they said?"
 "No, sir," said the soldier. "They said 'We haven't gone anywhere, have we?'"
 "And the north wall," Ligahrius said. "The south. Did they say anything of those."
 "No. Nothing."
 Ligahrius looked first to the north stair, then to the south, then to the gate where the soldier stood. He said nothing for a moment.
 "Bring me the men who saw the wall shots," Ligahrius said. "Whoever was nearest each one. I want them separately, and I want them now."
 The order went out through Rufeenus. Within a quarter hour Ligahrius had two men in front of him, in the record room off the praetorium, the Pryfectus standing by while Ligahrius asked the questions.
 Both accounts agreed on the key points. Neither man had seen anything at the tree line, or heard a sound before the sentry beside him fell. Both had the timing to within moments of each other. Neither could say from what distance the shot had come, only that it came from deep within in the tree line.
 Ligahrius set down his stylus and closed his eyes for a moment before he spoke again.
 "Two dead on the wall," he said. "Struck within the same breath, on two sides of this fort that face away from each other. A patrol taken on a third side, the same evening, by the same method that was used on the first attack. One survivor released, stripped, sent back walking." He looked up at the Pryfectus. "I've spent the last two days telling myself this was contained to the ground west your walls. I don't know what to think now."
 "Then it's not seven women in a forest," the Pryfectus said. "It's something that can stand at my own walls and put arrows through my sentries."
 "It's still seven women," Ligahrius said. "Or eight. I haven't changed my count. What's changed is where I believed the edge of their reach to be." He stood. "I know where their camp is. I've stood in it myself. And by every report I've gathered since I arrived, it hasn't moved in over two weeks."
 "You think that carelessness."
 "I think it confidence, possibly overconfidence," Ligahrius said. "This garrison has refused to set foot on that ground since well before I arrived. They've had two proofs now that nobody from this fort will follow them home. A band that bold, sitting that long in one place, isn't hiding from you. It's decided it doesn't need to." He paused. "Tonight, while every man in this yard is looking at his own dead, that camp is very likely unwatched. They've had no reason yet to think we'd dare attack."
 The Pryfectus looked at him for a long moment.
 "You're proposing we go to them."
 "Before sunrise tomorrow," Ligahrius said.
