CHARACTER PROFILE

NAME: Thana

PRESENTED TO READER: Thana
 Pronunciation: tHAH-nuh
 Origin: Greek
 Meaning: "death"
 Thana is the first of the eight introduced to us on the sand of the Kynegion arena in Byzantium. One of Servius's many gladiatrices, she had been taken by pirates from her hometown on the coast of Greece. She was fourteen at the time, from a family of good standing and modest means.
 As a child, she had excelled in her lessons, her exacting and analytical nature finding its true passion in arithmetica. This preternatural ability to solve problems instantly, using any means available, served her well in her role as gladiatrix, allowing her to walk victorious from eleven death matches. Over time, her ruthless calculating directness hardened into a smug, cynical outlook on life.
 In combat, she handles her xiphos with calculated dexterity, unafraid to step into close hand-to-hand bouts, prepared to fall back on her xiphidion if required. Even at a distance, she is deadly accurate with her akontia.

APPEARANCE: Olive skin, dark eyes. Long dark hair, tight braids on the left side, loose and flowing on the right — the shared signature hairstyle worn by all eight women. Lean, athletic build. A faint, near-permanent suggestion of a smirk at rest.

DRESS & EQUIPMENT: Bronze cuirass with lion-head motif at the belt, worn over a short tunic. Layered leather pteruges skirt. Single bronze vambrace on the sword arm. Bronze greaves with lion motifs, worn over leather sandals. Plain pendant at the throat. Xiphos in hand. Twin akontia carried across the back. Xiphidion at the hip as a secondary sidearm. Tall oval thureos shield, wood-faced with a bronze lion boss, carried when the fight calls for it.

BACKGROUND: Greek-born to a family of modest standing in a small coastal town. Taken at fourteen when pirates came ashore at night. Sold into gladiatorial slavery under Servius at the Kynegion in Byzantium. Survived eleven bouts. Escaped Byzantium alongside Toria and Tacita during the staged lion incident, killing pursuers during the breakout.

NATURAL TEMPERAMENT (DISC): C(d) — analytical, exacting, controlled, with an undercurrent of blunt directness.

PRESENTING PERSONALITY: Dry, ironic, needling. Tests people with pointed questions before deciding what she thinks of them. Wears an easy, settled arrogance and a near-permanent smirk. Deflates enthusiasm with flat correction. Taunts a helpless target in a calibrated, domestic register rather than a crude one, and stops once the target stops reacting. Behavior shaped by the arena, where plain calculation read as weakness and had to be dressed in bravado instead.


CHARACTER PROFILE

NAME: Toria

PRESENTED TO READER: Toria
 Pronunciation: toh-RREE-uh
 Origin: Iberian
 Meaning: "conqueror"
 Toria is the second of the eight we see, first shown to us as opponent of Thana, set to battle to the death, before Tacita intervenes. She has been under the brutal hand of Servius for longer than the others, having been taken as a small child when Roman soldiers were sent to quell unrest in her native province on the Iberian Peninsula. She remembers little more than the sound of her mother's voice being cut off mid-word, and the smoke that did not belong in that hillside village.
 Her outgoing, fun-loving nature was severely curtailed during her years at the Kynegion in Byzantium, forcing an underlying dominance to the fore. This served her well during those years, building in her a fiery spontaneity, a quick eye and a lethally fast hand.
 In combat, she will face down the toughest of enemies, her falcata leaving a trail of destruction. Through her hastiness, she is prone to put herself in harm's way, but rarely does she remain there long enough for harm to reach her. So ready is she to lead in close combat, that the spear she carries seldom serves any greater purpose than to simply be there if required.

APPEARANCE: Olive-brown, sun-deepened skin. Long black hair, tight braids on the right side, long and flowing on the left. Dark eyes, straight brows, flat-set mouth. Compact, athletic build — close-combat musculature through shoulders and forearms.

DRESS & EQUIPMENT: Bronze-and-leather cuirass, midriff bare, over a short leather corselet. Bronze pauldrons, overlapping lames. Bronze bracers, both forearms. Wide leather-and-bronze belt over a bronze-edged leather pteruges skirt. Bronze greaves, leather sandals, no helmet. Falcata carried at the hip, horse's-head pommel. Round shield and spear also carried per reference image.

BACKGROUND: Born in Iberia. Taken as a small child when Rome put down unrest in her province. Raised and trained a gladiatrix in Servius's Kynegion alongside Thana. Co-executed the Byzantium breakout.

NATURAL TEMPERAMENT (DISC): I(d) — outgoing, people-oriented; secondary dominance (forward, assertive drive).

PRESENTING PERSONALITY: Blunt, forward-driving, quick to anger. Acts before a plan is fully formed. Commits fully once committed, without pause to weigh the fight. Behavior shaped by history rather than by native temperament; the underlying I(d) surfaces in flashes — open admiration of others' competence, unguarded wistfulness, empathetic reading of an enemy's psychology — but is not her default presentation.

ROLE IN COMBAT: Direct assault. First through a door, first at a blocked path. No reconnaissance role shown in the text.


CHARACTER PROFILE

NAME: Tacita

PRESENTED TO READER: Tacita
 Pronunciation: TA-sih-tuh
 Origin: Roman
 Meaning: "silent" or "secret"
 Tacita is introduced in the Kynegion arena in Byzantium. Owned by Servius, she has been a gladiatrix there for three years, after being sold by her father to settle a debt.
 Although she was already a quiet girl by nature, the betrayal of paternal duty forced her to build an unbreakable resilience through silence. She has an unplumbed depth of emotion — a unique understanding of hardship that, though silent, does not refrain from offering her own peculiar form of compassion. She had always been steady and precise, but due to her past and her extreme self-discipline, these features have sharpened to lethality.
 Her role in combat is never on display. She is the silent steady hand holding the blade that doesn't so much appear as just suddenly exist at a throat. She acts as the silencer of the survivor, and the extractor of the hostage. Her gladius is more an extension of her presence than a weapon, when in close combat, and a handful of plumbatae carried at the belt are reserved for silencing from a distance.

APPEARANCE: Olive skin, dark eyes, heavy dark brows. Hair worn in the band's signature style, tight braids at the left temple, the rest long and loose. Solid build, not lean. Watchful at rest; expression gives nothing away.

DRESS & EQUIPMENT: Bronze cuirass over a short maroon tunic. Leather pteruges at the belt. Bronze vambrace on the sword arm. Bronze greaves. Plain leather sandals. Gladius at the hip. Plumbatae carried at the belt. Round shield, dark leather over bronze rim and boss. Compound-issue gladiatrix armor, worn rather than displayed.

BACKGROUND: Sold by her father to settle a debt. Three years a gladiatrix in Servius's Kynegion before the Byzantium breakout, which she planned and executed.

NATURAL TEMPERAMENT (DISC): S(c) — steady, accommodating, deliberate; secondary conscientiousness (precision, restraint).

PRESENTING PERSONALITY: Near-total silence. Speaks only when asked, in as few words as possible. Communicates through gesture — a look, a touch, a blade pointed in a direction — as often as through speech. Behavior shaped by history rather than by native temperament; the underlying S(c) surfaces in flashes (precision under pressure, quiet steadiness, restrained compassion) but is not her default presentation.

ROLE IN COMBAT: Closer, not opener. Finishes engagements already broken open by others. No described strikes or named motions in the text; rendered through outcome only.


CHARACTER PROFILE

NAME: Tigratavā

PRESENTED TO READER: Tigratavā
 Pronunciation: tee-grah-tah-VAH
 Origin: Sarmatian
 Meaning: "with the strength of an arrow"
 We first see Tigratavā as she tracks a Roman patrol on the Sarmatian steppes. Born into the Roxolani tribe, she is the sole survivor of her family, after her father and the forty men who rode with him were disarmed and killed by the Roman officers they had ridden with for the previous eleven years. The blame for a Roman raid on a village north of their territory was laid on them, and Tigratavā, who had been moving herds to the east at the time, returned to a burned camp and forty unburied dead. From that time on, she had fended for herself, surviving by pillaging passing Roman patrols and supply trains.
 Her natural analytical precision served her well through those lonely years, and her patient observation allowed her to choose her battles well. This same reserved caution has made her the ideal scout for the band.
 Tigratavā is often the one to open combat, precisely placing one of her trilobate arrow heads through the throat of an enemy, sending it from her composite recurve bow, and usually from concealment. When called upon to assist in close combat, she is no less adept at wielding her long sword.

APPEARANCE: Dark hair, braided with metal ornaments threaded through on the left side, worn loose past the shoulders on the right. Sharp-featured, still and watchful at rest. Lean, functional build.

DRESS & EQUIPMENT: Lamellar cuirass of hardened leather plates, bronze-edged and embossed with medallion motifs at the shoulders, chest, and belt — steppe cavalry construction, flexible at the joints. Baldric worn across the chest supporting the gorytos (combined bow-case and quiver) at the hip, arrows fletched and tipped with trilobate heads. Composite recurve bow carried in hand or unstrung across the back. Long sword worn at the belt as a secondary weapon. Leather greaves and boots, banded to match the cuirass.

BACKGROUND: Born to the Roxolani, one of the Sarmatian peoples. Her father led forty riders serving Rome as auxiliary cavalry for eleven years, paid in grain and land rights. After a Roman patrol raided a village north of their territory, the governor needed someone to blame; her father and his riders were called to a muster, disarmed, and killed. Tigratavā was moving the herd to summer pasture at the time and returned to a burned camp and forty unburied dead. She declined to join surviving family groups still treaty-bound to Rome. She spent three winters alone by choice, tracking and raiding Roman patrols, until she found Thana, Toria, and Tacita doing the same, badly, and joined them.

NATURAL TEMPERAMENT (DISC): C(s) — precise, analytical, reserved; secondary steadiness (patience, consistency).

PRESENTING PERSONALITY: Reserved, observant, exact. Speaks little and elaborates only when pressed. Corrects by realignment rather than contradiction, restating what's true instead of arguing against what's false. Consistently first to notice a threat or relevant detail. Values people by demonstrated worth rather than sentiment, and says so plainly.

ROLE IN COMBAT: Scout and ranged support. Reads terrain and enemy movement ahead of engagement; opens ambushes with the bow from concealment.


CHARACTER PROFILE

NAME: Toria
PRESENTED TO READER: Tiata
 Pronunciation: tee-AH-tuh
 Origin: Dacian
 Meaning: "daughter of the strong"
 Tiata is a larger-than-life mystery, first seen openly displaying her latest victim. The mystery is her past, which she has never cared to share, and has never been asked to reveal, however, hints of it are visible in her unmitigated vendetta against the Roman surveyor — any Roman surveyor. It hints at a past that has built in her a controlled yet bitter resentment of the Roman partitioning of the Dacian territory.
 Her self-sufficiency is predominant when we are introduced to her personal campsite, where everything is arranged decisively to serve its most practical use. She wears her dominance openly, yet her natural conscientiousness leads her to seek the opinions of others before making her final decisions. This habit of leading by listening softens her otherwise superior bearing, making of her a trusted and trusting unranked commander.
 In combat, her initial role is in planning. Her direct and decisive manner ensures everyone plays their part correctly. When it comes to the battlefield, her size and her two-handed falx leave nothing standing before her. So meticulous is she in her preparedness that she also carries a long sword at her left hip, though she rarely finds cause to use it.

APPEARANCE: Olive-brown, sun-deepened skin. Long black hair, tight braids on the right side, long and flowing on the left. Dark eyes, straight brows, flat-set mouth. Compact, athletic build — close-combat musculature through shoulders and forearms.

CHARACTER PROFILE

NAME: Tove

PRESENTED TO READER: Tove
 Pronunciation: TOH-veh
 Origin: Scandinavian
 Meaning: "beautiful thunder"
 Tove is first seen in the forest between the Iazyges pastures and the Hasdingi holdings, unbothered by an hour of being watched from the tree line. Rugii by birth, from a cold coastal homeland far to the north, she was taken by Gothic raiders and traded onward to Hasdingi hands. She allowed her captors to underestimate her until the opportunity presented itself for her to flee, and had been alone in the forest since.
 Tove's warmth and affability are her most disarming traits, inviting the band to join her on their first meeting and willingly sharing her meal. She's the first to welcome a newcomer, and the first to offer help to the wounded.
 In combat, Tove is willing to lead the strike, her raw focus and the brutality of her axe carrying the weight of her past into every battle. She discovered that revenge alone offered no relief to the pain of her past, and even her affability can be weaponised by using it as a patronising corrosion to an enemy iron soul.

APPEARANCE: Fair hair, tight braids on the right side, long and flowing on the left. Pale eyes, level gaze. Tall, broad build — strength held in reserve rather than displayed.

DRESS & EQUIPMENT: Layered leather over a dark underlayer, riveted and quilted panels reinforcing torso and forearms, wolf-fur collar at the throat. Wide leather belt. Dark leggings. Tall leather boots, creased from wear. Long-hafted, single-handed bearded axe, haft wrapped in worked leather — an early, heavier-headed forerunner to what would later be called a skeggøx. Large round wooden shield, rune-carved face, iron boss. Nothing ornamental.

BACKGROUND: Rugii by birth, from a cold coastal homeland in the far north Rome never conquered. Taken captive by Gothic raiders, traded onward to Hasdingi (Vandal) traders. Won her freedom by letting her captors underestimate her until their carelessness became exploitable. Alone in the forest since, until the band found her dressing a boar and she invited them to sit.

NATURAL TEMPERAMENT (DISC): I(s) — sociable, warm, steady; secondary steadiness (consistency, patience).

PRESENTING PERSONALITY: Matches her natural temperament closely — generous, direct, dryly humorous, unguarded. Answers hard questions about her own past without flinching. In a fight, fast and unshowy, a raw focus at odds with her fireside manner. Physically demonstrative — a hand on the shoulder, an embrace — toward whoever in the band is newest or most unsteady.

ROLE IN COMBAT: Front-line striker. Given tactical lead when trusted with it. Works ground-holding and terrain tasks alongside Thilda.


CHARACTER PROFILE

NAME: Thilda

PRESENTED TO READER: Thilda
 Pronunciation: THIL-duh
 Origin: Germanic
 Meaning: "strength in battle"
 Thilda comes onto view scouting along a stretch of border, when she comes across the campfire of the band of six women. Born to a people who held relatively resource-poor ground in Upper Germania, she fought first under self-important commanders, and later alone, coming to know both Rome's patient encroachment from the south and the raiding tribes pressing down from the north. We are not told what happened to her people other than that she had buried more of her own than she could remember.
 Whatever the hardships were that she faced during her early years, they had only served to strengthen and stabilise a quiet confidence in her own ability to read the landscape. The caution first seen in her covert observation of the others around their campfire was no less present in her bold approach, after deciding they were worth investigating.
 In combat, Thilda holds the line, her tower shield planted as solidly as herself, an immovable object against which seemingly unstoppable forces break themselves. She is unafraid to use her framea in direct attacks and will turn to the spatha when close combat requires it.

APPEARANCE: Fair skin, pale eyes, long hair between dark blonde and light brown. Tight braids on the right side, the rest left long and flowing. Tall, solid build. Weathered features.

DRESS & EQUIPMENT: Fitted leather vest and shoulder guards over a long-sleeved underlayer, fur-trimmed at the collar and shoulders. Fur-trimmed bracers and greaves. Hide-wrapped, cross-laced boots. Framea carried foremost, planted upright as a signal of restraint as much as a weapon. Spatha worn at the belt, drawn only once the spear's reach is lost. Tall hexagonal shield, iron boss and iron-banded rim over wood, built to anchor a line rather than move quickly with it.

BACKGROUND: Born to a people holding ground in Upper Germania — land no invader wanted for its resources, resented only for the people's refusal to disappear. That refusal cost her people, and cost her more than she has said. Served under men who gave orders without asking what the ground permitted; does not elaborate further. Scouted and fought alone along the Raetia/Upper Germania border for several years afterward, understanding both Rome's patient encroachment and the raiding tribes from the north. Found Tiata's band by their fire, watched an hour, then walked down in full view rather than by stealth. Accepted the same night; given a voice in planning within a day.

NATURAL TEMPERAMENT (DISC): S(i) — steady, accommodating, relationally warm; secondary influence (openness, ease with others).

PRESENTING PERSONALITY: Patient and watchful before committing to action or disclosure. Verifies ground, water, cover, and routes herself rather than accepting another's read. Steady and matter-of-fact under pressure. Proactive without being asked — volunteers for the next task as soon as the current one is finished. Speech is spare and declarative, laid out as option, consequence, likelihood rather than persuasion. Presentation built over her natural warmth by history rather than replacing it; the underlying ease surfaces occasionally, most clearly in her decision to end her isolation and approach the fire.

ROLE IN COMBAT: Ground-scout and sentry. Holds the line rather than opens it. No kill by her hand shown on the page.


CHARACTER PROFILE

NAME: Taran

PRESENTED TO READER: Taran
 Pronunciation: TAHRR-uhn
 Origin: Caledonian
 Meaning: "thunder"
 Taran is the last of the eight, first seen while still in captivity at Fort Lorch, standing silent by a wall as the Praefectus displays her to visiting officers as proof of Rome's control even beyond the frontiers. Taken from Caledonia as an exotic curiosity, she has spent three years treated, in her own words, like a hound that had learned to heel. She breaks her captivity the night a frightened soldier's account of an ambush reaches the household, lifting weapons from the Praefectus's own armoury before scaling the fort wall in the dead of night.
 From the little we are told about her life in Caledonia, we can see that her speed and dexterity was more than a match for a Roman soldier. This fiery-tempered nature never burned out during her captivity, and even the practice blades she was proffered were used to keep her skills sharp. Over the three years of captivity, she learned a unique cold patience that now acts as a faucet through which she channels her frenzy, however, it never chilled her natural warmth which resurfaces the moment she allows herself to relax into Tove's embrace.
 In combat, Taran appears to be the embodiment of a contradiction. She has the immense patience required to be a highly skilled archer, and other times, she's a screaming banshee who drops from overhead onto five armed men with her twin blades already flying. The tuagh she has at her belt has not yet seen action since Caledonia, but would no doubt prove as deadly in her hands as her other weapons.

APPEARANCE: Long red hair, tightly braided at the temple on her right side, worn loose and flowing on her left. Pale woad-blue markings across both cheekbones. Solid, athletic build.

DRESS & EQUIPMENT: Fitted, riveted brown leather cuirass over a wool tunic, fur trim at collar, shoulders, and hem. Wide leather belt. Fur-cuffed leather vambraces. Leg-bindings over trousers. Fur-topped leather boots. Twin blades (a little larger than daggers) worn at the hip. Yew bow carried slung across the back. Tuagh (throwing axe) carried at the belt.

BACKGROUND: Taken captive from Caledonia three years before the story's present (the Praefectus separately describes her as taken "in Britannia"). Held since in the Praefectus's household at Fort Lorch, in a chamber off his own quarters. Required to wear household-dictated dress, wear her hair loose, stand near the door at meals, and speak only when addressed. Paraded before visiting officers as proof of a tamed captive; demonstrates swordplay with a practice blade for these visitors, showing only enough skill to satisfy them. Her bow was confiscated without her knowledge and hung as a trophy above the Praefectus's own armory rack. Escapes after overhearing a soldier's account of an ambush (seven women, four Roman dead); takes twin blades and her own bow from the armory, then scales the wall using sentry-timing. Intercepted in the forest by Tacita, then questioned by Tiata; provisionally accepted pending proof, which the fort's continued silence the next morning supplies.

NATURAL TEMPERAMENT (DISC): D(i) — dominant, decisive; secondary influence (expressive, sociable).

PRESENTING PERSONALITY: Contained and plain-spoken under captivity and under the band's initial scrutiny — short, unembellished answers, waiting rather than acting unless required to perform. Behavior shaped by three years of conditioning rather than by native temperament. The underlying D(i) surfaces once secure with the band: decisive action taken on incomplete information (the escape itself), freer movement, open enjoyment of her own freedom, laughter and teasing, and a stated, undisguised personal motive distinct from the band's wider aims.

ROLE IN COMBAT: Holds an assigned flanking position to draw off enemy strength; kills at range with the yew bow under first real pressure without hesitation.
